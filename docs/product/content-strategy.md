# 03 — Content Strategy

## Core niche

Faceless, English-language Instagram Reels focused on **interesting, visually strong, tension-driven stories**.

## Positioning

This is not generic trivia.
This is not generic history.
This is **cinematic short-form storytelling**.

## Core content promise

Every Reel should make the viewer feel:
- curiosity immediately
- tension within seconds
- a satisfying reveal or unresolved mystery by the end

## Signature style decisions

To make the account recognizable, standardize the following:

- one narrator voice style
- one subtitle visual style
- one pacing pattern
- one music mood family
- one cover image style
- one ending style

## Recommended signature elements

### Narration tone
- calm
- serious
- cinematic
- controlled pacing

### Visual tone
- dark cinematic or muted documentary style
- atmospheric
- not cartoonish unless intentionally chosen

### Hook formula
Start with one of these patterns:
- a shocking fact
- a year/date + mystery
- “they found…”
- “no one could explain…”
- “what happened next…”

### Ending formula
Use one consistent ending family such as:
- “And no one ever discovered the truth.”
- “To this day, the mystery remains unsolved.”
- “What happened next has never been fully explained.”

## Content buckets

Use rotating content buckets so the feed stays coherent but not repetitive.

### Bucket 1 — maritime mysteries
Examples:
- lost ships
- abandoned vessels
- unexplained sinking events

### Bucket 2 — historical mysteries
Examples:
- unexplained deaths
- vanished expeditions
- unsolved discoveries

### Bucket 3 — survival stories
Examples:
- impossible rescues
- strange recoveries
- people found in unusual places

### Bucket 4 — archaeology and discovery
Examples:
- buried cities
- strange artifacts
- forgotten ruins

### Bucket 5 — legend vs reality
Examples:
- famous myths with factual anchors
- historical legends
- disputed stories with visual appeal

## Story selection rules

A story is a good candidate when it has at least 3 of the following:
- strong opening image
- clear tension
- strong curiosity gap
- memorable twist or ending
- visual scene potential
- reasonably defensible source support

## Story rejection rules

Reject stories that are:
- too abstract to visualize
- too dependent on specialist context
- low-confidence without labeling
- weak in hook strength
- too long and complex for a short Reel
- likely to trigger avoidable trust issues because of poor sourcing

## Reel structure template

### Version A — 30 to 40 seconds
- 0–2 sec: hook
- 2–8 sec: setup
- 8–22 sec: escalation
- 22–35 sec: reveal or unresolved twist
- 35–40 sec: closing line / follow cue

### Version B — 40 to 55 seconds
- 0–2 sec: hook
- 2–10 sec: setup
- 10–25 sec: details
- 25–42 sec: escalation / tension
- 42–55 sec: ending / mystery close

## Brand guardrails

Avoid:
- clickbait that completely misrepresents the story
- overlong context dumping
- robotic overly formal narration
- visuals that do not match the script
- excessive text density on screen

## CTA strategy

Use soft CTAs, not aggressive ones.

Examples:
- “Follow for more true stories like this.”
- “More real mysteries if you want part two.”
- “Follow for stories history never fully explained.”

## Long-term channel vision

The goal is to train the audience to expect:
- mystery
- cinematic visuals
- concise narration
- trustable storytelling
- addictive pacing
